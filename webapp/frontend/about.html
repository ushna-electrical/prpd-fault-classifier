About

Project summary

Early detection of partial discharge (PD), which indicates degradation of the insulation in medium-voltage (MV) cable systems, provides an opportunity to detect faults developing before they cause total system failure. Cable joints and terminations have a higher sensitivity to PD than the rest of the cable system due to being constructed in the field by workers, rather than in a factory. The quality of the worker's construction will affect the sensitivity. In addition, defects that may contribute to PD in electrical equipment include poor grounding connections, voids or air bubbles between the various components used to form the joint or termination, and/or contaminants on the surfaces of components prior to assembly. However, these defects cannot be identified by analyzing the PD patterns.

This thesis studies how these installation defects appear in phase-resolved partial discharge (PRPD) patterns measured on real MV cable joints and terminations, with the goal of building an image-based dataset for AI-based fault classification. Measurements were taken with a BSS PDM-600 PD monitoring system using three channels: a UHF antenna (343 MHz–3 GHz) and two IEC-compliant channels, one with a high-frequency current transformer (HFCT, 1–52 MHz bandwidth) and one with a coupling capacitor. A reference measurement was recorded under healthy, de-energized conditions before each fault was introduced, tested, and reversed.

Research objectives

The work is organised around six objectives:

  1. To measure the PRPD patterns of defects created in a laboratory on MV cable joints and terminations, using a three-channel monitoring system with one UHF antenna and two IEC channels (one HFCT and one coupling capacitor), and to compare every fault with a reference measurement on the same cable.
  2. To build a labelled PRPD image dataset of the fault classes and three sensing channels, screened for images that contain only the noise floor and split by recording session so that no recording appears in more than one of the training, validation and test sets.
  3. To establish a transfer-learning baseline with MobileNetV3-Large and to test whether a CNN can recognise the fault type from a PRPD image clearly better than chance, using only image transformations that are physically meaningful for the phase-resolved representation.
  4. To compare approaches for the small and imbalanced classes (GAN-based synthetic images, few-shot metric learning and a supervised ResNet-18 working on the recovered PRPD matrix, individually and as an ensemble) and to quantify their effect on each fault class.
  5. To determine, fault by fault, which of the three sensing channels gives the most reliable classifier, instead of fixing one channel in advance.
  6. To calibrate the output probabilities of the selected classifier so that its reported confidence matches the observed frequency of correct predictions.

Source: thesis §1.3 “Research Objectives” (chapters/01_introduction.tex)

Model and method overview

The work is organised as a chain of steps in which each step produces the input of the next. The recordings are first converted into a screened, session-split image dataset. A transfer-learning baseline then shows how far standard methods get on this data. Because the dataset is small and imbalanced, three further approaches are examined: synthetic images from a class-conditional GAN, few-shot metric learning, and a ResNet-18 working on the PRPD matrix recovered from the image, which is finally combined with the few-shot model in an ensemble. The three sensing channels are then compared, and the confidence values of the selected model are checked for calibration.

The main contributions are:

  • a documented laboratory campaign of twelve fault classes on MV cable joints and terminations, created as reversible, artificial defects that correspond to errors that can also occur in service, recorded simultaneously on a UHF channel and two IEC channels, with each fault compared against a reference on the same cable;
  • a set of 1,387 measurement files and a PRPD image dataset of 11,083 images formed from 1,195 of them, together with a slicing tool that converts the recordings into images and an automated image-quality audit that removed 4,453 images showing nothing but the noise floor, leaving 6,630 images from 424 recording sessions;
  • a leakage-safe evaluation protocol in which whole recording sessions, never individual images, are assigned to training, validation and test sets;
  • a systematic comparison of transfer learning, class weighting, Siamese and prototypical few-shot learning, a CAE-ACGAN generator, a ResNet-18 working on the recovered PRPD matrix and an ensemble of the two best models; on the extended Sensor-2 dataset the ensemble reaches a test macro-F1 of 0.884 over twelve labels;
  • a fault-level analysis of defects which are similar to other categories and remain confusable (especially termination-surface grounding faults versus air gaps at the cable-head interface), interpreted as a physical finding about pattern similarity rather than as a modelling defect.

Source: thesis §1.4 “Approach and Contributions” (chapters/01_introduction.tex)

Limitations

Effective sample size.
The image dataset has 424 retained sessions (378 on Sensor 2), and F1 and F5i have only four and five. On Sensor 2 the test sets of F1 and F5i contain a single session each, and those of F10, F12 and F13 four to five. Per-class results for the smallest classes rest on one recording and can change strongly with a different split. F14 is not yet part of the image dataset.
Calibration era.
The HFCT calibration changed during the campaign, and the fault classes are almost perfectly confounded with the calibration era. A classifier could exploit the amplitude scale, particularly for F3 and F1.
Cable confound.
Several classes were recorded on a single cable (F1, F4, F5i, F8 and F9 on Cable 01). Cable-specific background patterns may contribute to their recognition or confusion; cable identity was deliberately not modelled.
Channel labelling.
The measurement log notes several recordings in which Sensors 2 and 3 were switched. If these files were not corrected, some images carry the wrong channel label.
Rendered images.
The classifiers work on rendered PNG plots with a per-image logarithmic colour scale, not on raw event data. Absolute counts cannot be recovered, and the rendering itself is part of the model input.
Pooled baseline manifest.
The pooled MobileNetV3-Large baseline was run on the manifest version before the quality audit, so its numbers are not strictly comparable with the Sensor-2 results.
Duplicated F4 images.
In the extended Sensor-2 split, all F4 validation and test images are copies of training images. The F4 score on this split is not a held-out estimate, and the macro-F1 without F4 (0.875 for the ensemble) is the more reliable figure until the split is regenerated without the copies.
Two datasets.
Model development and the ablation study used the initial Sensor-2 dataset, the final evaluation the extended one. Results of the two are not directly comparable, because the test sets differ.
Statistical evidence.
The advantage of session-disjoint episodes is consistent in direction but not significant with three seeds (p = 0.10).
Per-sensor models and confidence bands.
The Sensor-1 and Sensor-3 models were trained with a single seed, and several Sensor-1 test sets contain only one to five images, so their per-class values are not reliable on their own. The accuracies of the confidence bands of the field tool were measured on the same test split as all other results and need confirmation on independent recordings.
Laboratory data.
All defects are artificial and reversible, created on four cable samples in one laboratory. Laboratory accuracy does not transfer automatically to field data.

Source: thesis §10.5 “Threats to Validity” (chapters/10_results.tex)

Why this tool exists

This tool turns the thesis's trained models into something you can actually use: upload a PRPD plot and get a predicted fault, a confidence score, and a plain-language read on how much to trust it. It's built around the Coupling Capacitor ensemble — a prototypical network plus a ResNet-18 — the best-performing of the three channels, covering the ten fault classes all three share.

It isn't part of the thesis itself. It simply packages the thesis's trained models, calibration results and fault taxonomy into something clickable: a classify page, a fault library, a results dashboard, and a one-click PDF report. And like the thesis, it only ever answers with one of those ten classes — it doesn't try to flag images of conditions outside that scope.
